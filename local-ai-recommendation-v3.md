# Private AI for Research Papers: Laptop Recommendation

**Prepared for:** Head of Faculty · **Date:** September 2026 · **Budget ceiling:** €5,000 including VAT (approval not yet confirmed) · **Status:** For decision

> ## TL;DR
> - **Public papers:** use a cloud AI service (ideally under an institutional agreement). No new hardware needed.
> - **Confidential or personal material:** use a **Windows laptop with an AMD Ryzen AI Max+ chip and lots of memory**. **HP ZBook Ultra G1a** is the first choice: 128GB if the quote fits €5k, otherwise 64GB.
> - **Dell** is a sound vendor, but its in-budget laptops currently have too little AI memory for this job. Revisit when Dell's RTX Spark laptops are reviewed.
> - **Peer-review and grant-assessment material:** check the publisher's or funder's rules first. Many restrict AI even offline.
> - **Nothing is signed off** until it passes a test on real, non-confidential papers.

## 1. The use case

> **TL;DR:** Summarise and compare research papers, including figures and tables, some of which are confidential.

| Task | Example |
|---|---|
| Summarise a paper | Research question, method, results and limitations in a fixed format |
| Compare papers | A side-by-side table showing where papers agree and disagree |
| Read figures and tables | Results that appear only in charts or tables |
| Handle sensitive material | Unpublished drafts, student dissertations, internal documents |

---

## 2. Which tool for which material

> **TL;DR:** Ask two questions: **may it leave the university?** and **is AI allowed for this task at all?** Public → cloud. Confidential or personal → the laptop. Peer review and grant assessment → check the rules first.

| Category | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Public** | Published papers, preprints, public reports | Cloud AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini), preferably under an **institutional agreement** |
| 🟡 **Confidential** | Your own unpublished drafts, internal documents | **The local AI laptop**, or an institutional cloud service **if university policy approves it** |
| 🟠 **Personal data** | Student dissertations, interview transcripts, anything identifying people | **The local AI laptop**, following university data-protection (GDPR) guidance |
| 🔴 **AI restricted** | Manuscripts under peer review, grant proposals being assessed | **Check the publisher's or funder's rules first.** [NIH](https://www.grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-23-149.html), [ERC](https://erc.europa.eu/news-events/news/erc-clarifies-limits-ai-use-grant-evaluation), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-referees/policies-and-processes) and [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals) restrict or prohibit AI in review, **online or offline** |

**About cloud plans:** personal or free plans may use conversations to train future models unless this is switched off. Institutional plans generally do not ([OpenAI business terms](https://openai.com/business-data/), for example). "No training" is only one safeguard, so university approval is still needed.

## 3. What local AI does and does not guarantee

> **TL;DR:** Local AI keeps documents away from AI companies. It does not, on its own, make the laptop secure or GDPR-compliant.

- ✅ **It does** process documents on the laptop instead of sending them to an AI company.
- ❌ **It does not, by itself** stop other software (cloud sync, backups, updates) from communicating, or make processing personal data automatically compliant.

The technical colleague will configure the laptop so documents stay on it (section 11).

## 5. Recommendation

> **TL;DR:** Buy the **HP ZBook Ultra G1a**: 128GB if the quote is within €5k, otherwise 64GB. If the purchase can wait, check RTX Spark reviews first.

1. **First choice: HP ZBook Ultra G1a, 128GB.** It holds every candidate model, including the largest text model (gpt-oss-120b). It is a business product with business support, and it keeps Windows.
2. **If 128GB exceeds €5k: HP ZBook Ultra G1a, 64GB.** It runs the figure-reading models well but not the largest text model. For summarising papers, that is an acceptable trade-off.
3. **If the purchase can wait two to three months:** compare against **RTX Spark laptops** (including Dell's) once independent reviews appear.
4. **Only if a change to Mac is welcome:** MacBook Pro M5 Max (48GB). It is faster, but holds smaller models.

Two limitations to accept:

- Very long documents are slow to process. Summarising one paper at a time (section 7) keeps this manageable.
- Local models are less capable than the best cloud services, which is why public papers still go to the cloud.

## 6. Dell: suitability

> **TL;DR:** Dell is a strong vendor for a university, but **no current Dell laptop combines enough AI memory with a price under €5k**. Watch Dell's RTX Spark laptops.

**Why Dell is attractive:**

- Dell is commonly used on public-sector and university purchasing frameworks, with business support (ProSupport) and IT management tools. **Check whether the university has a Dell agreement**, since that can change prices and procurement effort.
- Dell offers Windows or Ubuntu Linux on its Pro Max workstations.

**Why Dell is not the first choice today:**

- No AMD Ryzen AI Max+ laptop appears in Dell's current range; its AMD Pro Max laptops use the Ryzen AI 300 series, which lacks the large shared memory. *Confidence: moderate, so worth confirming with a Dell representative.*
- Dell's AI-capable laptops use **separate NVIDIA graphics with their own, smaller memory**:
  - **RTX PRO 3000, 12GB:** the Pro Max 16 Plus was listed at about $3,597 (US). That suits small models only, and the figure-reading models considered here need roughly 20GB.
  - **RTX PRO 5000, 24GB:** independent lab tests rank it the **fastest laptop for local AI**. The Pro Max 18 Plus was tested at a list price of about $9,245, far over budget, and 24GB is still below the HP's capacity.

**When Dell becomes a strong option:**

- **Dell RTX Spark laptops**, once priced and reviewed: large shared memory plus NVIDIA software, from a vendor the university may already buy from.
- **Dell Pro Max with GB10:** Dell's version of the NVIDIA desk box (see the [footnote](#13-footnote-desk-side-ai-boxes)).

## 7. How you will use it

> **TL;DR:** One paper at a time, using a saved template. Then compare the summaries. Always check numbers against the cited page.

### Step 1: Summarise each paper separately

```
Summarise this paper using exactly these headings:
1. Research question
2. Method and sample (include sample size)
3. Key results (include numbers and the table or figure they come from)
4. Limitations stated by the authors
5. Limitations not stated by the authors
6. One-sentence takeaway
For every result, give the page number where it appears.
If something is not in the paper, write "not reported". Do not guess.
```

### Step 2: Save each summary

Keep them together, one section per paper.

### Step 3: Compare the summaries

```
Below are structured summaries of [N] papers.
Compare them in a table: research question, method, sample size,
main result, and limitations. Then list where they agree,
where they disagree, and what might explain the disagreement.
```

### Step 4: Check before relying on it

AI tools, local and cloud, sometimes state things that are not in the paper. **Check every number used against the page cited.**

**Why one paper at a time?** AI models tend to overlook material in the middle of very long inputs ([Liu et al., 2024](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00638/119630/)).

## 8. Concepts behind the choice

> **TL;DR:** Memory size decides **which** models fit. Memory speed decides **how fast** answers appear. Compression and "mixture of experts" designs let large models fit and run on a laptop. For summarising papers, the **wait before the first word** matters more than how fast the answer types out.

| Concept | Analogy | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| **Memory capacity** | The size of your desk | The model and the document being read must both fit |
| **Memory speed (bandwidth)** | How fast you can read | Mostly sets answer speed; **processing power** matters more when first reading a long document |
| **Parameters** | Number of pages in a reference work | Model size (e.g. "35B"); bigger is often more capable, but design matters |
| **Quantisation** | A photo saved with stronger compression | About a quarter of the memory, with a small quality loss that varies by model ([Dettmers & Zettlemoyer, 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/dettmers23a.html)) |
| **Mixture of experts (MoE)** | A large faculty where only the relevant specialists answer each question | The whole model is stored, but only a small part is used per word, so it runs fast for its size |

**Windows and AI memory on AMD laptops:** on a 128GB Ryzen AI Max+ system, Windows can dedicate up to **96GB** to the graphics unit ([AMD](https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2025/experience-unparalleled-performance-with-the-amd-ryzen.html)). Linux can expose more ([AMD ROCm docs](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/docs-7.2.0/how-to/system-optimization/strixhalo.html)). The recommended models fit within 96GB when loaded one at a time, so **Windows is fine** for this use case.

### Two speeds: typing speed and waiting time

AI speed is usually quoted in **tokens per second** (a token is roughly three-quarters of a word). For this use case, two different speeds matter:

| Speed | What you experience | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **Generation speed** (tokens per second) | How fast the answer types out | Average silent reading is about 238 words per minute ([Brysbaert, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2019.104047)), or roughly 5 tokens per second. Anything above about 15 tokens per second already outpaces reading |
| **Time to first word** | The wait before anything appears | **The real bottleneck.** A paper is a long input (about 10,000–15,000 tokens) that must be read before the answer starts |

**Illustrative waiting time for a 15,000-token paper** (arithmetic, not benchmarks):

| Reading-in speed | Wait before the summary starts |
|---|---|
| 300 tokens/s | ~50 seconds |
| 1,000 tokens/s | ~15 seconds |
| 3,000 tokens/s | ~5 seconds |

Verified figures for this waiting time are not available for these machines and models, which is why the [acceptance test](#10-acceptance-test) measures it.

## 9. Software, models and PDFs

> **TL;DR:** A simple chat app, two or three tested models, and a PDF tool that keeps tables intact. The final choice of models comes from the acceptance test.

### Software

| Component | Suggested tool | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Chat app | [LM Studio](https://lmstudio.ai/) or [AnythingLLM](https://anythingllm.com/) | Where documents are uploaded and prompts saved |
| PDF extraction | [Docling](https://github.com/docling-project/docling) or [Marker](https://github.com/datalab-to/marker) | Converts papers to text and **tables**, keeping page numbers |

The technical colleague should verify licences and network behaviour before deployment.

### PDFs are a pipeline, not a single input

A PDF contains text, images and drawn graphics, so it is handled in stages:

1. **Parse** the PDF, keeping layout and page numbers.
2. **Extract tables as tables**, not as pictures.
3. **Send key figures** to an image-reading model.
4. **Summarise with evidence:** every claim tied to a page, table or figure.

### Candidate models (benchmark, do not fix in advance)

| Model | Approx. size (4-bit) | Reads figures? | Fits 64GB laptop? | Fits 128GB laptop? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Gemma 4](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4) 26B MoE (preferred) | ~16–18GB (est.) | ✅ Yes | ✅ | ✅ |
| [Gemma 4](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4) 31B dense | ~19–20GB | ✅ Yes | ✅ | ✅ |
| [Qwen3.5-35B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) | ~20–24GB | ✅ Yes (verify version) | ✅ | ✅ |
| [gpt-oss-120b](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss/) | ~63GB | ❌ Text only | ❌ | ✅ |

**Prefer the 26B MoE over the 31B dense on the AMD laptops.** The dense model reads all its weights for every word, so on these laptops it is estimated at only about 10–12 tokens per second, close to reading speed. The MoE version reads only its active part and is estimated at roughly 6–8 times faster. For comparison, a similar MoE model (Qwen3 30B-A3B) measured about 86 tokens per second on this chip, and gpt-oss-120b about 53 tokens per second (both on Linux; Windows may differ).

Cloud models are generally stronger on hard reasoning and on reading figures, but the gap varies by task. The acceptance test provides the evidence.

## 10. Acceptance test

> **TL;DR:** Before sign-off, run eight types of non-confidential paper through each candidate model and check the numbers, tables, figures, page references and speed.

**Test papers:** single-column, two-column, scanned, figure-heavy, table-heavy, mathematics-heavy, colour-coded figures, and long (30–50 pages).

| Measure | How |
|---|---|
| Numerical accuracy | Every number checked against the paper |
| Table accuracy | Extracted values compared with the original |
| Figure accuracy | Claims about figures checked by a subject expert |
| Page references | Cited pages correct? |
| Speed | Time to first word, generation speed (tokens per second), total time |
| Reliability | Memory use and failures logged |

**Suggested speed thresholds** (proposed for this project, not an established standard):

| Measure | Pass |
|---|---|
| Time to first word, typical paper (10–20 pages) | Under 60 seconds |
| Generation speed | At least 15 tokens per second |

Run the same papers through the institutional cloud service, if available, as a quality baseline.

## 11. Security and governance

> **TL;DR:** IT sign-off, an encrypted disk, no cloud sync of documents, individual login, controlled updates, and a data-protection assessment if personal data is involved.

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Approval | IT security sign-off; a data-protection impact assessment (DPIA) if personal data will be processed |
| Storage | Full-disk encryption (BitLocker); AI documents kept **outside** OneDrive or other cloud-synced folders |
| Accounts | Individual university account; no shared administrator access |
| Updates | Windows patched normally; AI software and models updated deliberately by the technical colleague |
| Telemetry | Disabled in the AI tools where configurable; outbound traffic checked during setup |
| Retention | Uploaded documents and chat history deleted according to university rules |
| Physical | Laptop treated as a device holding confidential data (locked when unattended; report if lost) |

**Copyright:** summarising papers the university has lawful access to is generally covered for research purposes under EU text-and-data-mining exceptions (Directive 2019/790, Articles 3–4), although their application to AI summarisation is not fully settled. Some publisher licences restrict uploading full texts to third-party services, which matters more for the cloud route.

## 12. Risks and next steps

> **TL;DR:** Confirm the budget, get HP quotes (128GB and 64GB), check for an institutional cloud licence, get IT sign-off, then test.

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Prices rise further | Obtain quotes promptly; 64GB as fallback |
| Local models underperform on figures | Test two image-reading models; the cloud remains for public papers |
| New models run poorly on AMD at first | Stay with tested models; updates managed by the technical colleague |
| AI misstates results | Page-referenced prompts and mandatory checking |
| AI used on restricted material | Section 2 rules included in the handover |

**Actions:**

1. **Confirm the budget**, and whether VAT is recoverable.
2. **Check for an institutional cloud AI agreement.** It covers public papers immediately.
3. **Obtain quotes:** HP ZBook Ultra G1a at 128GB and 64GB; ASUS ROG Flow Z13 at 128GB for comparison; Dell, if the university has a Dell agreement.
4. **Get IT security and data-protection input.**
5. **Run the acceptance test** before sign-off.
6. **Revisit RTX Spark laptops** once independently reviewed.

## 14. Glossary

> **TL;DR:** The handful of terms used in this document, in plain language.

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Local AI** | AI that processes documents on your own device instead of sending them to an AI company; it does not on its own guarantee the device never communicates over the network |
| **Parameters** | The size of a model; bigger is often more capable, but design matters |
| **Quantisation** | Compressing a model to use less memory, with a small quality loss |
| **Mixture of experts (MoE)** | A design where only a small part of a large model works on each word, so it runs fast for its size |
| **Unified memory** | Memory shared by the processor and graphics, letting large models fit on a laptop |
| **Multimodal** | Able to read images (e.g. figures) as well as text |
| **Open-weight model** | A model whose files are published and can run on your own device |
